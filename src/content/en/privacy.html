Your account

To open stories and listen, you need an account; without one you can go through the introduction and see the day’s headlines. When you create an account, we process your email address, your password and, if you enter it, your name. Google’s Firebase Authentication stores them; we never see your password. You can also use Sign in with Apple (on iPhone and iPad) or Sign in with Google. Apple or Google then shares your name, your email address (Apple may give a private relay address) and a sign-in token with us. With Google, Firebase Authentication also stores the web address of your Google profile photo; the app doesn’t use or show it. During Google sign-in, Google’s sign-in software may also use your IP address to estimate your approximate location, to protect the sign-in against fraud.

When you create an account with email and password, we send a 6-digit code to confirm the address is yours. The code comes from {emailHello} through Google Workspace and is valid for 10 minutes. We don’t store the code itself: only a one-way summary of it and how many times it was tried, for 7 days at most. Password reset emails come from Firebase Authentication, in the name of {domain}.

Before you sign in, the app may create a random guest ID (a Firebase anonymous account) for features that need one. It isn’t linked to your name or email, but it is still personal data, because what is stored under it can be linked back to your phone. If you then create an account, your guest ID becomes your account ID. If you sign in to an account you already have, the app stops using the guest ID. A guest ID that isn’t used for 7 days is deleted automatically.

What syncs to your account

When you are signed in with an account (not as a guest), the app keeps the following in Google Cloud Firestore so it follows you to your other devices. Only you can read it.

  • Preferences: your topics, languages, story depth, text size, listening settings and notification choices.
  • Saved stories (up to 50), stories you recently read or listened to (up to 30) and your listening queue (up to 20). These are copies of the stories inside the app.
  • Topics and sources you follow or mute.
  • Your own copy of your votes and poll answers, and the stories you reported.
  • Your word list and My questions (up to 1,500 entries): each word or concept with the sentence and headline where you found it. When you ask by voice what something means (for example “what is …?”), the concept and the answer are added here.
  • Today’s listening progress: which stories you heard, how long you listened and how many questions you asked. Only the number of questions is kept, not the questions.
  • Your subscription status and today’s count of free listening time (see Subscription and payment). Only our server writes these.

Votes and reports

When you react to a story or answer a poll, your vote is stored with your account ID, so that it counts once and you can change it. Other people see only the totals. Votes are deleted with the story, 7 days after the story’s day.

When you report a problem with a story or an answer, we store the story, the reason you picked and the note (up to 500 characters). If you report a voice answer, the app fills the note with your question and the answer so we can see what went wrong; you can change or delete it before you send. The report isn’t linked to your ID, and it is deleted after 7 days. Please don’t put personal information in the note: because it isn’t linked to you, we can’t find it earlier to delete it.

Microphone and questions

Push-to-talk questions

While a briefing plays, you can hold the microphone button and ask a question. The app records only while you hold the button, for up to 15 seconds. It sends the recording, together with the sentence that was playing, to our server. Our server sends them to the Google Gemini API, which works out your question and writes an answer. Google’s text-to-speech then reads the answer in the briefing’s voice.

Before your first voice question, the app tells you that your voice goes to Google’s AI and asks you to agree. Nothing is recorded or sent until you do.

We don’t store the recording, and we don’t write what you said or the answer to our logs. There are two exceptions, both in your hands: if you report an answer, the report keeps your question and the answer for 7 days (see Votes and reports); and when you ask what something means, the concept and the answer are saved to My questions in your Library and sync with your account until you delete it (see What syncs to your account). Our logs keep only numbers, such as the length of the answer and how long it took. Your voice is not used to identify you.

We use the Gemini API as a paid service. Under Google’s terms for paid services, Google doesn’t use this data to improve its products; it keeps prompts and answers for a limited time only to detect misuse and to meet legal obligations.

Voice commands

If you turn them on, your phone’s own speech recogniser understands the short commands you say while a briefing plays (“stop”, “next”, “faster”). Recognition happens on your phone; the audio doesn’t go to us or to any other company, and it isn’t stored. The microphone listens only while the app is on screen and a briefing is open, or the Today screen is showing; the player, the mini player and Today say “Listening”. You can turn voice commands off in Profile › Reading and listening.

Ready-made questions

Some questions are buttons, such as asking for more detail on a story. Their answers are about the story, not about you, so we save them with the story and reuse them for everyone.

Your phone asks for your permission before the app can use the microphone, and for speech recognition before voice commands. You can turn it off at any time in your phone’s settings.

How the news is made

Our server collects stories from news outlets’ public feeds, and reads the opening paragraphs of their pages to compare how outlets report the same story. It sends them to OpenAI to write the summaries and to Google to read them aloud. These requests contain only news text, none of your data. Stories and their audio files are deleted from our servers 7 days after the day they were published.

Your personal briefing is put together on your phone from the shared edition, so your topics aren’t sent to any AI service.

News images load directly from the publishers’ servers, and source links open the publisher’s page in a browser inside the app. Those publishers can see your IP address and apply their own privacy policies.

On your phone

  • A cache of story audio, in a temporary folder that your phone may clear.
  • Today’s count of free listening time. If you subscribe, the Today audio downloaded for offline listening also sits in the cache folder and isn’t backed up.
  • Your sign-in session: on iPhone and iPad in the Keychain, where it can remain after you delete the app; on Android in the app’s private storage.
  • An offline copy of your synced data, kept by Firestore.

When you sign out or delete your account, the app removes your lists, votes, words and progress from the phone. It keeps your app settings (topics, languages, text size) so the app keeps working. Deleting the app removes them.

For usage measurement and crash reports the app keeps a random ID and the records waiting to be sent on your phone. Your choice to turn them off is kept on the phone too and isn’t cleared when you sign out.

To protect our servers from misuse, the app uses Firebase App Check, with Apple’s DeviceCheck on iPhone and iPad and Google Play Integrity on Android, to confirm that requests come from the genuine app. We don’t store device identifiers.

Notifications

Only if you allow notifications, your phone subscribes to topics (such as your morning briefing time or major developments in your edition) through Firebase Cloud Messaging, and on iPhone and iPad also Apple’s push service. We send to topics, not to you personally, and we don’t store a device token. You can turn notifications off in the app’s Profile or in your phone’s settings.

Usage limits and server logs

To keep the service fair and affordable, we count how many questions you ask and how many story audios you have generated each day. Each counter holds your account ID, the date and a number. While a story’s audio is being made, the job is recorded with your ID. Counters and job records are deleted after 7 days, and at once when you delete your account.

Our servers run on Google Cloud in Belgium (europe-west1). Google Cloud keeps request logs with IP addresses and app and device details for 7 days. Our own log lines contain numbers, not account IDs.

Subscription and payment

The free version lets you listen for a set time each day. To count it, the app keeps how many seconds you listened today on your phone and in your account: only today’s number, overwritten the next day, with no history. It isn’t counted for subscribers.

If you subscribe to Premium, Apple takes the payment on the App Store and Google on Google Play; we never see your card or payment details. We confirm the purchase and your subscription status with RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives your account ID and the store’s purchase record; it doesn’t receive your email, your name or your device’s advertising identifiers. Your subscription’s current status (which product, when it ends, which store, whether it renews) is kept in your account and deleted when the subscription ends. Only our server writes it.

If you subscribe, the audio of your Today list is downloaded to your phone so you can listen offline. It sits in your phone’s cache folder and isn’t backed up. It is deleted on a new day, when you sign out, when you delete your account or when your subscription ends, and within 7 days at the latest.

Deleting your account doesn’t end the subscription; cancel it separately in the App Store or Google Play subscription settings. Purchase, trial, cancellation and refund terms are in the Terms of Use.

How long we keep data

Your account and what you keep in it stay until you delete the account. Usage measurement is kept for 90 days. Everything else is deleted within 7 days: a job runs every night and removes what is older.

  • Account and synced data: until you delete your account.
  • Votes: deleted with the story, 7 days after the story’s day, or earlier if you delete your account.
  • Guest IDs: deleted after 7 days without use. This includes a guest ID left behind when you signed in to an existing account.
  • Reports: 7 days.
  • Email verification codes: the code itself isn’t stored; its summary and the number of tries for 7 days at most, at once when you delete your account.
  • Daily usage counters and audio job records: 7 days; deleted at once when you delete your account.
  • Subscription: its current status while it lasts; your RevenueCat record until you delete your account. The free listening count is today’s only; offline audio 7 days at most. Apple and Google keep purchase records under their own policies and legal obligations.
  • Usage measurement: 90 days; 2 months in Google Analytics. Your account’s usage summary (first and last day of use, totals) until you delete your account; without an account, 90 days after last use. Daily totals that aren’t tied to anyone (e.g. how many people listened that day) are kept.
  • Crash reports: 90 days.
  • Ad data: on Google’s own schedule; we don’t keep it.
  • Voice recordings: not stored. A question and its answer are kept only as described above: in a report (7 days) or in My questions (until you delete your account).
  • Stories, story audio and ready-made answers: 7 days after the story’s day.
  • Server logs: 7 days. They contain no account IDs.
  • Emails you send us: as long as we need to deal with your message. If you ask us to tell you when the app is out, we keep your address until we have done so, then delete it.
